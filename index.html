About my work

Understanding people.
Building more supportive
digital spaces.

My research brings together human–computer interaction, computational social science, and digital health. I explore peer support, spiritual care, and meaning-making in online communities.

I combine interviews, co-design workshops, and surveys with natural language processing and computational analysis to understand people’s experiences and translate them into thoughtful design.

Computational social scienceDigital health

Support, re-engagement, and meaning-making online

Examining how community responses relate to continued engagement in mental health and spirituality communities on Reddit.

More about this research +

I lead a project integrating archived conversations, theory-informed qualitative annotation, and an NLP classification pipeline to study how people reinterpret difficult experiences. The work combines conversation reconstruction, human annotation protocols, and statistical modeling.

Co-designOnline spiritual care

Designing communities that support care

Working with spiritual care professionals to explore how online communities can support meaningful care, reflection, and peer connection.

More about this research +

I led co-design workshops with 24 spiritual care professionals and conducted interviews and qualitative analysis to inform community governance and user experience. I also collaborated with CaringBridge’s product and design teams on “Light a Candle,” a feature for expressing support without words, described in my CV as in development.

Community-centered designHuman–AI interaction

Tools shaped by the communities that use them

Co-designing a Reddit bot with r/compSocial to support knowledge-sharing, topic discovery, and academic discussion.

More about this research +

Through contextual inquiry, a user survey, and two rounds of user testing, I developed and refined keyword subscriptions, topic notifications, and research-paper recommendations. Moderator and community feedback guided iterative design and evaluation.

Mixed methodsPrivacy & security

How people make sense of digital security

Investigating how people’s mental models of security threats have evolved across computers, smartphones, and connected devices.

More about this research +

This mixed-methods replication adapts a foundational 2010 study to modern security concerns. Surveys, in-depth interviews, and thematic analysis help uncover how people understand risks and make everyday security decisions.
